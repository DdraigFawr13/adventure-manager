26 October 2026 · before plan approval (M0)

Plan review

Owner's request (2026-10-26): review the whole plan for efficient lean design, the gameplay loop and fun, codebase set-up that avoids missteps, and standards and practices. Method: two independent reviewers (D-107) read the docs cold, one for fun and one for lean production; the main session reviewed codebase and standards. Every finding below was checked at the cited line; three reviewer claims were corrected or dropped (end of page). Written as proposals; your decisions are under Outcome.

Impact: High changes what gets built or risks the project; Medium shapes it; Low is tidy-up.

Outcome (26 October 2026)

You decided five items and asked me to go with my recommendations for the rest:

  • Hours: 10–20 a week; timeline restated to about 31–46 months (D-108).
  • Launch cut: the lean cut (D-109).
  • Engine: web-first; Godot only as a fallback (D-110).
  • Gates: R1 failing twice stops building for a redesign; 50% over runs a cut review (D-111).
  • Late game: 3–4 small crises at launch; larger crises and legacy goals after (D-112).
  • Everything else adopted as recommended (D-113 design and production, D-114 engine foundations and standards) and applied to the plan.

Verdict

The plan holds together well: event log as the spine, determinism, content as data, gated milestones, and art money waiting for proof are all sound and lean. Budget and schedule arithmetic reconcile. The weak spots are (1) the plan is sized for a small team but the owner chose solo, (2) the heart of the game, how a quest actually resolves, is not designed yet, (3) the mid and late game have no fresh goals and a cheap dominant strategy is possible, and (4) four engine foundations that are cheap now and very expensive later are not written down.

Top 10 actions (proposed, in order)

  1. Re-plan for solo (L1, L2): restate the timeline for a solo builder and adopt a smaller launch cut, keeping everything as data so it grows after.
  2. Write "resolution v0" (F1): half a page on how party, plan, stats and site become events and odds; first M1 deliverable.
  3. Keep the simulation core (L3): build it as the production engine package from day one; only prototype screens are throwaway.
  4. Settle the engine with less work (L3): new evidence (Story-Generator is TypeScript and canvas) favours web; build Godot only if the web ledger or map test fails.
  5. Four engine foundations in §11 (C1–C4): values that carry their own breakdown and cause; named random streams kept in the state; a command log for replaying any bug; an event-log retention rule.
  6. Price every safe choice (F2, F5): make caution, delegation and skipping winter cost something visible, and test it in the headless simulator.
  7. Give years 10–30 goals (F3, F6): mid-career goals and a world-events and crises budget in §7.
  8. Kill and cut triggers, owner hours (L4): write weekly hours down; set slip triggers that apply the cut list; define "stop".
  9. Marketing and tester plan (L5): wishlist targets per gate; where the 30–50 and 200+ testers come from.
  10. A short standards file before the first line of code (S1): conventions, boundaries enforced by lint, definition of done.

2. Gameplay loop and fun

  • F1
    How a quest resolves is not designed. CONCEPT.md:33 says quests "simulate on their own"; no section in CONCEPT or PLAN turns party, plan, stats and site into odds and events. R1 (PLAN.md:536) tests whether watching is fun before the thing watched exists. Proposal: "resolution v0" as the first M1 deliverable, with tunable numbers in data.
    High
  • F2
    Caution's price is not weighed. PLAN.md:308 "cautious retreat thresholds and good knowledge roughly halve them [deaths]". The cost exists (abandoned contracts lose fee and renown, PLAN.md:273) but is never set against halved deaths. If it is small, "always cautious" is the solved game. Proposal: an R2 check that cautious and bold play both win sometimes.
    High
  • F3
    Progression runs out around year 10. Top tier in 8–12 years (PLAN.md:331), full facilities in "8–12 years" (PLAN.md:377), guildmaster careers 20–30 years. Late pressure exists (PLAN.md:334) but no new goals. Proposal: mid-career goals (legacy projects, a larger ladder or compound pulled earlier, a region-scale threat).
    High
  • F4
    The routine week has almost no decision. PLAN.md:106 "take or decline contracts, send a party with its saved plan, continue". If R1 fails, the loop is "approve, continue, read". Proposal: one standing tension most weeks (a deadline clash, a promise due, a rival bidding on the same contract); state the "one more week" hook.
    High
  • F5
    Delegation is likely nearly free. R6 passes at "10–25% better ... delegation never ruinous" (PLAN.md:541); the true cost (the guildmaster stops growing) is slow and unseen. Proposal: show the cost when delegating; give staff visible mistakes.
    Medium
  • F6
    No world events or story arcs at launch. CONCEPT.md:49 promises "story arcs and world crises"; the §7 content list has none (searched "story arc", "crisis", "world event": only PLAN.md:180 "seeded world events"). Proposal: a small crisis category in §7 (plague, war, a great beast).
    Medium
  • F7
    Repetition across a long save. About 750 quests over 25 years on 20 quest templates, and ~625 key moments on 60 (from PLAN.md:86, :468, :469); R8 tests only report wording. Proposal: combine site × monster × complication × faction state, and test shape repetition too.
    Medium
  • F8
    Bad starts can spiral, with rubber-banding ruled out. PLAN.md:287 "two in a row means a loan or a sale"; PLAN.md:280 "no hidden bonuses or rubber-banding". Proposal: visible recovery levers (patron emergency grant with strings, charity contracts, debt for service); test a "bad two years" seed set.
    Medium
  • F9
    Winter: a quarter of the year, about a tenth of the play. PLAN.md:94 weeks 37–48, "~15 min", time-skip by default; wages keep running. It also lists "academy", which is not at launch (D-070). Proposal: make winter the planning season (budget, building, training plans) with a cash model in R2; drop "academy" from the line.
    Medium
  • F10
    Only the mid guild is budgeted. Friction and deaths are set for 3 parties (PLAN.md:266); a top guild runs 4–8 (CONCEPT.md:189). Proposal: a top-guild row in §2f and in R3.
    Medium
  • F11
    Thin first hour for veterans and little self-expression. No guaranteed season-one story beat beyond the prologue; expression is mostly the charter. Proposal: one seeded early beat (a rival's poaching try); party names and banners.
    Low

3. Codebase set-up

  • C1
    Values that explain themselves need an engine type from day one. PLAN.md:414 "Hovering or focusing any number shows how it was made"; PLAN.md:508 "Every change records its cause". §11 does not say how. Proposal: every game number is a value with its parts and a cause id, built into the core types before any rule; retrofitting it later touches every system.
    High
  • C2
    Random numbers: one seed is not enough. PLAN.md:663 "A seeded generator ... passed in"; PLAN.md:640 simulate(state, command, seed). Proposal: named streams per system, derived from (seed, name), kept in the state, as Story-Generator does (PLAN.md:749). Then adding one roll in recruitment does not reshuffle every quest, so balance runs and bug replays stay comparable.
    High
  • C3
    Replays for bug reports. Determinism makes this almost free: playtest saves keep the commands since the last daily snapshot, so any tester's bug replays exactly on the developer's machine. Not in §11 or §15 (searched "replay", "command log"). For a solo developer this is the biggest time-saver available.
    High
  • C4
    The event log grows without limit. The log feeds Trace, histories and Chronicle (PLAN.md:646); saves must load in under 1 s at 30 years (PLAN.md:687). No retention rule (searched "retention", "compact"). Proposal: full detail for recent years, summaries for older ones, Chronicle-worthy events kept forever.
    Medium
  • C5
    Package boundaries are described, not enforced. Proposal: a lint rule that the engine imports nothing from ui or shell and never touches the clock or Math.random; a short AGENTS.md per package for AI coding sessions.
    Medium
  • C6
    Text templates have no grammar yet. No rules for variables, pronouns, plurals or conditional lines (searched "pronoun", "plural": none). Proposal: pick the template format before R8 writes lines; one schema source generates both validation and types.
    Medium
  • C7
    Screen-reader check was deferred to §11 and never done. PLAN.md:430 "(assumption to check in §11)". Proposal: add it to the engine test's judging list; the two engines differ here.
    Low
  • C8
    UI labels as well as world text in string files from day one, so translation (D-104) stays cheap.
    Low

4. Standards and practices

  • S1
    No coding standard yet. Proposal: one short file written at M1 start: strict types, no any, pure engine, a test for every rule, naming, small files, and a definition of done per feature (VER-07).
    Medium
  • S2
    Invariant checks. Proposal: engine assertions (money conserved, no negative health, every event has a cause) run in every balance sweep; plus golden-file tests for generated text.
    Medium
  • S3
    Pre-approve the stack's dependency list when the engine is chosen, so SAFE-03 is one decision, not a stop per package.
    Low
  • S4
    Rules specs written just in time: a half-page spec per system just before it is coded, not all up front (lean).
    Low
