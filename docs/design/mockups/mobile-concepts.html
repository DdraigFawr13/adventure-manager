Pocket Guild: mobile concepts

Yes, it can be done with every feature kept. The engine, content, saves and writing carry over unchanged; the work is in the screens.

The game is TypeScript and web tech (D-110), so a phone or tablet build wraps the same code in a mobile app shell instead of Electron. Nothing in the simulation needs a mouse. What does not fit a phone is the desk's density: the roster is built to show 25+ rows at 1366 px (R4), and a phone shows 7 to 12. Each concept below answers that differently.

Launch stays Windows only (D-106) unless you decide otherwise. Every hour figure on this page is an estimate (REP-01), for one developer working with AI help, on top of the Windows game.

Landscape · tablet

A. Tablet desk

The Windows desk as it stands, on a tablet held sideways. The largest iPad is 1366 × 1024 points, the same width as the PC minimum (D-091), so the roster keeps its 25+ rows. Smaller tablets (1180 × 820) fold the letter tray into a tab, which STYLE §3 already does below 1366 px.

Keeps

  • Every screen and the R4 density, nearly unchanged.
  • The kingdom map and quest board at full size; pinch to zoom.
  • Long sessions on a sofa: a tablet suits a 2-hour season.

Bends

  • Hover has no touch equivalent: every breakdown needs a tap or long-press.
  • Keys R, F and C need on-screen buttons.
  • Smaller tablets are below 1366 px: tighter columns, tray as a tab.
  • No phone support at all.

Work (estimate)

250–550 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Touch pass over ~25 screens, 1180 px layout: 60–150 h

About 3–13 months at 10–20 h/wk.

Landscape · phone

B. Pocket desk

The desk layout kept, squeezed onto a phone held sideways (844 × 390). Ledger tabs shrink to an icon rail, the top bar to one line, and the letter tray slides in from the right. Ledgers show 7 rows and page through column sets.

Keeps

  • One mental model with the PC game: same tabs, same top bar, same map home.
  • The map and quest board suit a wide screen.
  • The same layout works on tablets.

Bends

  • 7 rows, not 25: the R4 fact test fails by design. Finding facts relies on Find, sort and filters.
  • 390 points of height is cramped for letters and conversations.
  • Two-handed play only; no one-handed commute play.
  • Text at 150% (D-077) breaks most ledgers.

Work (estimate)

440–900 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Re-lay every dense screen for 390 px height, column sets, slide-over tray: 250–500 h

About 5–21 months at 10–20 h/wk.

Portrait · phone

C. Ledger book

A phone-first redesign, held upright and played with one thumb. Five tabs along the bottom, Continue as a large wax seal in thumb reach, every list a stack you tap into. Tablets keep concept A, so this is a second interface beside the desk, not a replacement.

Keeps

  • Every system, including the map, quest board, conversations and the Chronicle.
  • Letters, reports and the person card read better upright than on the PC.
  • One-handed play in short bursts; the daily autosave (D-033) makes stopping anywhere safe.

Bends

  • A different navigation model from the PC desk: two interfaces to keep in step.
  • Map and quest board reframed for a tall screen; the 3:2 March shows a third at a time.
  • Finance and ranking ledgers need new compact layouts.
  • The friction budget may need a phone setting: 100 stops a year (D-068) is a lot of taps.

Work (estimate)

690–1,400 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • ~25 screens redesigned at 20–40 h each: 500–1,000 h

About 8–33 months at 10–20 h/wk. Tablets use A, which adds 60–150 h if both ship.

Both orientations · phone

D. Turn the book

The phone is held upright for people and words (desk, letters, conversations, the person card, key moments) and turned sideways for ledgers, the kingdom map and the quest board. A hint invites the turn; nothing forces it.

Keeps

  • Each screen gets the shape it reads best in: letters tall, ledgers and maps wide.
  • Sideways screens share most of their layout with concept B and the tablet.

Bends

  • Two layouts for any screen a player may open both ways (roster, contracts): the most design and test work.
  • Rotation must keep scroll place, selection and open sheets: a common source of bugs.
  • Some players lock rotation; every sideways screen still needs an upright fallback.

Work (estimate)

600–1,210 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • ~15 wide screens: 170–330 h
  • ~10 upright screens: 200–400 h
  • Rotation and state: 40–80 h

About 7–28 months at 10–20 h/wk.

Money and machines, to recheck before any decision: an Apple developer account (about $99 a year), Google Play (about $25 once), both stores take about 15% under their small-developer programmes. iOS builds need a Mac (Apple's tools run only on macOS): buy one or rent one in the cloud. The engine needs no change: it is pure TypeScript with no clock or mouse in it (GAME-01, GAME-02).

My recommendation

  1. Keep Windows first (D-106). Even the cheapest concept adds 3–13 months to a 31–46 month plan. A mobile build before launch would be a second product before the first one exists.
  2. Plan for A after launch, then decide on C from sales. The tablet desk is the PC game with touch, so it is the cheapest way to learn whether mobile players want this game. C is the best phone game, but it is a second interface that taxes every later screen by 40–60%.
  3. Two cheap habits now that keep the door open (about 10–20 hours over the build, estimate): every hover breakdown also opens on click or tap, which also helps keyboard and screen-reader players (D-110) and a later Steam Deck build; and screens use flexible layout parts rather than fixed 1366 px positions.
  4. Skip B and D. B gives up the desk's density without gaining one-handed play. D costs as much as C and adds rotation bugs.

Left out: a phone companion that reads letters and answers stops for a PC save. It needs a server to sync saves (a backend, SAFE-03), breaks the offline promise (CONCEPT §14), and lets the phone and PC disagree about an Ironman save.

Basis for the estimates: about 25 launch screens (PLAN §6 ledgers plus map, quest board, letters, person card and set pieces), 20–40 hours to redesign and test one screen for a new layout, and 43–87 hours a month at 10–20 hours a week. They are judgements, not measurements; the mock-ups here took no position on art cost, which stays the same art (woodcut parts scale to any screen).
