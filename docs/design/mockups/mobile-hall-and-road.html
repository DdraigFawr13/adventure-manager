The concept · portrait

Hall and Road

Two places and two companions. The hall is home; tap the gate (or the March tab) to step outside onto the map. The satchel and the clerk are tabs you can reach from anywhere. Continue is the parchment handle at the top of the hall and the map: pull it down to unroll the next day.

Hall ⇄ March by tab or through the gate · Satchel and Ask from anywhere · pull down at the top to continue · the ledgers open from your study or from any clerk's answer.

Strengths

  • Each piece does one job: places for seeing, letters for deciding, the clerk for finding.
  • Four tabs, so nothing is more than one tap away.
  • Pillars 1 and 4 are on screen at all times: people inside, the region outside.

Weaknesses

  • Two kinds of navigation (tabs and walking through the gate) to learn.
  • The hall needs the compound's design (D-075) much earlier than planned.
  • Most art of any round: hall rooms and town views on top of PLAN §12.

Work (estimate)

1,380–2,780 h

  • Base, satchel and ledgers: 590–1,200 h
  • Clerk: 150–300 h · pull: 40–80 h
  • Hall: 350–700 h · map as navigation: 250–500 h
  • Art: about $9,000–30,000 more

About 16–65 months at 10–20 h/wk.

Variation 1 · portrait · no tabs

One long zoom

The hall is not a separate screen: it is the deepest zoom of the map. Pinch out from your study to the guildhall's cutaway, out again to the streets of Ashford, and out again to the whole March. There are no tabs. The satchel is a wax seal that floats in the corner, the clerk is a question bar across the top, and the pull handle sits at every zoom level.

Pinch in: March → Ashford → the hall → a room · seal button for letters · question bar for the clerk · pull to continue at every level.

Strengths

  • One rule to learn: pinch to go in or out. Very phone-native.
  • The hall sits in the world, so the town and the region feel continuous with home.
  • The cleanest screen of all: no tab bar.

Weaknesses

  • Getting from a far contract to the infirmary is three pinches; the clerk must carry fast travel.
  • The zoom between map and building is hard to make smooth; a hidden cut may be needed.
  • New players may not find things without tabs.

Work (estimate)

1,480–2,980 h

  • The concept: 1,380–2,780 h
  • One camera across map, town and hall; transitions: +100–200 h

About 17–69 months.

Variation 2 · portrait · one screen

Upstairs, downstairs

The map and the hall share one screen: the March above, the hall below, joined by the gate. The seam between them is the pull handle. When a party sets out you see them leave through the gate and walk onto the road above; when they come home they walk back in. Drag the seam to give either half more room.

One home screen, split · drag the seam to resize, pull it down to continue · satchel and clerk as tabs.

Strengths

  • Inside and outside at a glance: who is home, who is out, what is waiting.
  • Parties leaving and returning is a small, felt moment every week (Pillar 2).
  • Fewest screens to build of the five.

Weaknesses

  • Each half is small: the hall shows its busiest rooms only, the map one shire.
  • Busy: two illustrations at once can crowd the old-press calm.

Work (estimate)

1,460–2,940 h

  • The concept: 1,380–2,780 h
  • Split layout, resizable seam, gate-to-road walking: +80–160 h

About 17–68 months.

Variation 3 · landscape · phones and tablets

The open book

Held sideways, the phone becomes a book open at a spread: the hall on the left page, the map on the right. Letters stick out of the top like papers tucked between pages. Continue is a page turn: drag the bottom-right corner to turn to the next day. The same spread fills a tablet, so one design serves both.

Left page: the hall · right page: the map · tucked letters along the top · the clerk on a ribbon bookmark · turn the corner to continue · a ledger opens across both pages.

Strengths

  • Serves tablets too, so round one's A comes almost free.
  • Wide pages suit the 3:2 March (D-134) and dense ledgers.
  • The page turn fits the old-press look better than any other Continue.

Weaknesses

  • Two hands on a phone; no one-thumb play on the bus.
  • A 390-point-tall page is cramped for letters; they open full screen.

Work (estimate)

1,300–2,650 h

  • The concept, re-laid for a spread: about the same
  • Saves the separate tablet pass and some ledger re-layout: −80–130 h

About 15–62 months; the cheapest of the five.

Variation 4 · portrait · told by the clerk

The morning briefing

The clerk is the front door. Each time the calendar stops, the clerk walks you through what happened, with small views of the hall and the map inside the messages: “Two in the infirmary, here they are.” You answer with quick replies or open the full hall, map or letter from any message. The hall and the map stay one tap away as places.

Briefing first · tap a picture to open the hall or map there · replies as chips or sentences · pull to continue at the end of a briefing.

Strengths

  • The gentlest learning curve: someone explains each stop (helps the first season, D-076).
  • The clerk's voice (STYLE §9b) gives the phone game a character.
  • Every message comes from the event log and the report grammar (D-114).

Weaknesses

  • Feels like a chat app; veterans may want to skip straight to the places.
  • A clerk who explains everything risks reading as a tutorial forever; needs a “short briefings” setting.

Work (estimate)

1,580–3,180 h

  • The concept: 1,380–2,780 h
  • Briefing writer, inline views, quick replies: +200–400 h

About 18–74 months.

If the guildhall and the living map are built for Windows first (the compound, D-075; the map workshop, D-144), the phone version reuses their art and logic and the hours drop by about 300–600 (estimate). Extra art for hall rooms (about $6,000–20,000) and town views (about $3,000–10,000) is on top of PLAN §14b.

My read

  1. Variation 1 for phones, Variation 3 for tablets. They share every piece: the zoom from March to hall works upright, the spread uses the same hall and map sideways. Together they cost little more than either alone (about +150–300 h, estimate).
  2. Build the hall and the map for Windows first. Both serve the PC game's pillars too, and that is what makes any of these affordable later.
  3. The cost is real: even the cheapest option is about 15–62 months at your pace, close to half the Windows plan again. This stays an after-launch direction unless you decide otherwise (D-106).
