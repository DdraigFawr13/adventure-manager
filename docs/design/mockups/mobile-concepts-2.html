Exploration · round two · not a decision

Pocket Guild: wilder ideas

Seven ideas that stop treating the phone as a small desk and ask what a guildmaster would actually carry in a pocket.

Round one (four concepts) shrank the desk. These ideas change how you play instead: by letter, by asking, by pulling time forward, by walking the hall, by zooming the map, by dealing cards, or by letting the game run while the phone sleeps. Each keeps every system; some hide the ledgers one layer down.

Hours are estimates (REP-01) for one developer with AI help, on top of the Windows game, and include the same 190–400 h shared base as round one unless marked as an add-on. Months are at 10–20 h a week.

Portrait · letters first

E. The satchel

FM's inbox taken all the way. The phone is the guildmaster's satchel: patrons, criers, staff and adventurers write to you, and you answer. Orders are written as a reply where each underlined word is a choice, so briefing a party reads like a letter and uses the same plan levers as the PC screen. The ledgers still exist, as the clerk's enclosures.

Why it fits

  • The game already writes letters (D-043, report grammar D-114); this makes them the controls.
  • Reads well upright, one thumb, in short bursts.
  • Pillar 1: people speak to you in their own voices.

Risks

  • Depth is one tap further away: Pillar 5 relies on the enclosures being good ledgers.
  • Many letters a week could feel like chores; delegation must thin them.
  • Every system needs a "letter form" as well as a screen.

Work (estimate)

590–1,200 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Sentence-builder replies for ~15 order types, letter forms for each system, enclosures: 400–800 h

About 7–28 months.

Worth stealing even on PC: the sentence-builder brief. It shows the whole plan as one readable sentence.

Add-on · any layout

F. Ask the clerk

The phone can't show 25 rows, so let the player ask for the 6 that matter. A question box understands a fixed set of question shapes (who, which, how many, what did it cost, filtered by condition, class, line, wage, party, contract dates, feelings) and answers with a short note and the matching rows. It is a plain offline parser over the game's own data, not an AI; the optional AI flavour (D-017) could only reword the note.

Why it fits

  • Turns the phone's weakness (few rows) into speed: the answer, not the haystack.
  • The clerk is staff with a voice (STYLE §9b), so it stays in-world.
  • Your R4 test asks for facts in under 10 s; a question box might help on PC as well.

Risks

  • A question the parser doesn't know feels broken; suggestions must lead.
  • Not a whole interface: it needs a layout around it.

Work (estimate)

150–300 h

  • ~40 question shapes, the answer notes, results as ledgers: 150–300 h
  • On top of another layout's hours

About 2–7 months.

Portrait · time first

G. The unrolling scroll

Continue becomes a gesture: pull the parchment down to unroll the next day, or keep pulling to run on to the next stop. The whole game is a running chronicle with today at the top; stops are sealed entries you open; everything else scrolls past as the guild's story. Over years the scroll becomes the Chronicle (CONCEPT §31) without extra work.

Why it fits

  • The FM loop (continue, stop, respond) as one physical motion.
  • Pillar 3: history piles up under your thumb, so the guild's story is always visible.
  • Every line comes from the event log already (D-091).

Risks

  • Still needs ledgers, contracts and the board as separate screens (from round one's C).
  • Easy to pull too far; the pull must stop hard at every stop.
  • Changes the fixed top bar (STYLE §3) on phone.

Work (estimate)

540–1,100 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Scroll, pull-to-continue, entries for every event kind, plus slim ledgers: 350–700 h

About 6–26 months.

Worth stealing anywhere: pull-to-continue as the phone's Continue button. About 40–80 h on top of any portrait layout.

Portrait · place first

H. The guildhall

A tall phone is the shape of a building in cross-section. The home screen is the guildhall itself: your study opens the ledgers, the infirmary shows who is in bed, the tavern shows who is drinking with whom, the gate is where recruits and the road out begin. Building upgrades (CONCEPT §9) add rooms you can see.

Why it fits

  • Pillar 1 made visible: people in rooms, friendships at the same table, the injured in beds.
  • Upgrades are felt, not just numbers.
  • Strong store screenshot; easy to read at a glance.

Risks

  • Pulls the compound idea (D-075, Later) forward and needs its design.
  • Art: ~12 rooms in two or three upgrade states, and small standees: about $6,000–20,000 more art (estimate), on top of PLAN §14b.
  • Easy to slide into a Fallout Shelter look that fights the old-press desk.

Work (estimate)

690–1,400 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • The hall, room screens, people moving between rooms, plus slim ledgers: 500–1,000 h

About 8–33 months, plus the art.

Either orientation · map first

I. The living map

One continuous zoom instead of tabs. Pinch out to the whole March (factions, rivals, crises), in to a shire (contracts as wax seals, your parties on the roads), in to Ashford (the smith, the temple, the market), and in once more to the guildhall, which opens the ledgers. Where you are on the map is where you are in the game.

Why it fits

  • Pillar 4: the region is always under your fingers, and changes show where they happen.
  • Pinch and pan are what phones do best.
  • Reuses the kingdom map and the "View on map" idea (D-142).

Risks

  • The map art must carry four zoom levels; towns need their own drawn views.
  • Finding a person is slow by map; needs F (Ask the clerk) or a roster shortcut.
  • Phone GPU: the map test gave 44–48 fps on software rendering; real phones untested.

Work (estimate)

590–1,200 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Semantic zoom, town views, map pins for every system, slim ledgers: 400–800 h

About 7–28 months, plus town art.

Portrait · cards first

J. The war table

Adventurers, contracts, items and staff become cards in the woodcut style. Your free adventurers are a hand at the bottom; drag one onto a contract to put them in the party, and the card shows what changes: the chance everyone comes home, chemistry, who complains. Kit cards go onto people the same way (D-143 already draws gear as small cards).

Why it fits

  • Tabletop feel (D-004) in the hand, not just on the board.
  • Drag and drop is natural on touch and quick for party building.
  • Odds shown are the engine's preview, so the UI never computes a rule (GAME-02).

Risks

  • Card games invite gamey readings: deck building, rarity. Must stay people, not loot (WS-05).
  • A hand of 30 is too many; needs filters or F.
  • Showing odds may make the game feel solved; PLAN may want them as words, not percents.

Work (estimate)

540–1,100 h

  • Shared base: 190–400 h
  • Card layouts for 4 kinds, drag and drop, live preview, slim ledgers: 350–700 h

About 6–26 months.

Worth stealing on PC too: drag adventurers onto a contract with the preview updating live.

Wildcard · add-on

K. While you sleep

An optional away mode: when you close the game, the calendar keeps going at a slow real-time pace (for example an in-game day per real hour), parties travel and fight, and anything that would stop the calendar waits for you as a notification. Open the app and the scroll shows what happened. Notifications are local, so no server is needed. The engine is unchanged: real time is just another injected clock (GAME-01).

Why it might work

  • Phone-native: the guild lives on between commutes.
  • A year at one day an hour is about 14 real days, so seasons last weeks, not evenings.

Why it is risky

  • Changes the settled pace (D-064, D-066) and friction budget (D-068).
  • Pillar 2 "you plan, they bleed" is about deaths you were present for; a death at 3 a.m. may feel unfair.
  • Pulls toward idle-game design, which the target player (PLAN §1b) doesn't want.

Work (estimate)

200–400 h + rebalance

  • Away clock, local notifications, "while you were away" summary: 200–400 h
  • Rebalancing for real-time pace: not estimated; needs design first

About 2–9 months plus the rebalance.

What I would do with these

  1. The best phone game is a mix, not one idea. The satchel (E) as home, Ask the clerk (F) for finding facts, and pull-to-continue from the scroll (G). Letters, questions and time are what a phone does well; the ledgers stay one tap down for depth. As a whole that is round one's C with a different heart: about 780–1,600 h (estimate: E 590–1,200, F 150–300, pull-to-continue 40–80), a little more than C, because the letter forms come on top of the ledgers.
  2. Some ideas pay off on the PC first. F might help your R4 fact test now (round 1 had no answers under 10 s). The sentence brief (E) and dragging people onto contracts (J) suit the desk too. Building them for Windows makes a later phone version cheaper.
  3. The guildhall (H) and the living map (I) are art bets. Both look great and serve the pillars, but they need art the plan hasn't budgeted. They fit best when the compound (D-075) and the map workshop (D-144) are designed anyway.
  4. Leave K as a curiosity. It changes the game more than the platform.

Basis for the estimates: as round one (about 25 launch screens, 20–40 h per screen redesign, 43–87 h a month at 10–20 h a week), plus my judgement for the new parts (the question parser, the sentence builder, semantic zoom, card drag). All are estimates, not measurements. Names, letters and numbers in the mock-ups are invented examples in the game's voice.
