Ancestries of the March

Three directions for the five launch ancestries (PLAN §7a, Q7a). Names, ages and hooks are first drafts to show the direction; nothing here is final. "Prime" is when attributes peak and "decline" when they start to fall (PLAN §5a).

A · Familiar folk, our own names

Humans plus four peoples every fantasy player reads at a glance (stout hill folk, long-lived wood folk, small folk, tusked folk), renamed and given a March twist.

The fen and farm people of the March. Most of any roster.

Small, quick, sociable folk of the market towns.

Prime

Decline
from 60
Names
Pim Tallow, Rosie Brack

Hook: lift party morale; hard to scare, easy to hurt.

familiar

Grael

Tusked frontier clans who trade, raid and hire out their swords.

Prime
20–30
Decline
from 34
Names
Urska Two-Fires, Drog Marrowbone

Hook: strongest early; some factions distrust them.

GoodA roster reads instantly; tabletop players know the roles (D-063 secondary group).
CostLeast distinctive; easy to read as a generic fantasy game.
RiskMust avoid any Pathfinder names or traits (D-002); the shapes are folklore, the details must be ours.

Slow, broad people of the old woods, part bark, part breath.

Decline

Names
Old Haw, Rowan-under-Hill

new

Warm-skinned herders of the eastern ash moors, at home near fire.

Prime
20–30
Decline
from 33
Names
Kesra Embry, Tamsin Cole

Hook: resist fire and cold; restless in walls, love the open moor.

GoodStrongest identity; every ancestry ties into a place, faction or monster family (WS-05).
CostEvery ancestry must be taught; more art invention for portraits (STYLE §7).
RiskA roster is harder to read in the first hours.
